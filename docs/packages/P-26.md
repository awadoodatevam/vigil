### 6.5 `dev/fakes/` (Section 11.3)

One image `vigil/dev-fakes:local` (`dev/fakes/Dockerfile`, `ubuntu:24.04`, Python 3.12 from Ubuntu, standard library only, BP-R-04), one program `dev/fakes/fakes.py` started with `FAKE_ROLE` (`jira`, `teams`, `claude`, `openai`, `ollama-llm`, `ollama-embed`), listening on port 8000 (11434 for the two Ollama roles), keeping everything in memory. Common to every role: `GET /_fakes/calls` → the calls so far as a JSON array of `{"at", "method", "path", "body"}` (bodies as received; no header recorded); requests to `/_fakes/` paths are not recorded; an unknown path 404 `{"error": "not found"}`; one log line per call (method, path, status, milliseconds). "Token counts" are the characters of the text divided by 4, rounded up.

- **`jira`** answers the calls of `JiraConnector` (AR-K-14, P-21; complete list) in the forms of `dev/fakes/recordings/jira/`: `POST /rest/api/3/issue` (keys `NOC-1`, `NOC-2`, … in creation order, status `To Do`, the labels and the ADF description kept); `GET /rest/api/3/search/jql` (by the `labels` term of the JQL the connector writes); `GET /rest/api/3/issue/{key}?fields=status`; `POST /rest/api/3/issue/{key}/comment`; `GET /rest/api/3/issue/{key}/transitions` (always the three transitions of the recordings: `11` To Do, `21` In Progress, `31` Done) and `POST` with any of them (that status, 204). Plus `GET /_fakes/issues` (every issue with its labels, comments and status).
- **`teams`**: `POST` on any path records the body and answers 202 with an empty body.
- **`claude`** (`POST /v1/messages`), **`openai`** (`POST /v1/responses`; `POST /v1/embeddings` with `ollama-embed`'s vectors in the OpenAI form and `usage.prompt_tokens`, `usage.total_tokens`) and **`ollama-llm`** (`POST /api/chat`) answer in their recorded forms (`dev/fakes/recordings/claude/`, `openai/`, `local-llm/`) with token counts. The schema is read from `output_config.format.schema` (Claude), `text.format.schema` (OpenAI) or `format` (Ollama), the user message from `messages[0].content`, `input` and the `messages` entry with role `user`. The task is told by the output schema (`likely_cause` in its properties: SL-T-1; `summary`: SL-T-2); any other request answers 400 in the provider's error form. The parts are read from the user message as `AiConnector` renders them: a header `^### (.+) \((Alert data|Service context|Diagnostic output|Runbook text|Incident history|Incident record)\)$`, then the text up to the next header; the hypothesis from the part `Hypothesis` with `^Root-cause hypothesis: (.*) \(([^()]*)\), kind `; runbook parts are those of category `Runbook text`, labelled by their key.
  - SL-T-1: `likely_cause` "{hypothesis name} ({component key}) is the likely cause: {the text of part `E1`, cut to 200}." — or "{hypothesis name} ({component key}) is the likely cause." without `E1`, or "No hypothesis was supplied." without a hypothesis; `hypothesis_verdict` `confirms` with a hypothesis, else `not_applicable`; `evidence_labels` `["E1"]` when `E1` exists, else `[]`; `uncertainty` "Whether other components are affected independently."; `runbooks_cited` `["RB-DB-01"]` when it was supplied, else the first supplied key, else `[]`. With `E1` and a supplied runbook the answer passes PM-M17-03 (`OutputChecks`); without them it is refused as the product refuses it (`no evidence reference`, `no runbook cited`), which is the behaviour a test of that case expects.
  - SL-T-2: `summary` "{hypothesis name}: {the text of part `Alerts`}. {the text after `Likely cause: ` in part `Investigation` up to the end of that line, else `No AI investigation was accepted.`}", cut to 600 characters (the limit of `SYSTEM_SL_T_2`); without a hypothesis, the text from `Alerts` on.
- **`ollama-embed`** (`POST /api/embed`): one vector per input of 64 numbers computed exactly as `TestEmbedding.vector` (the k-th number from the first two bytes of SHA-256 of `{text}#{k}`, scaled to −0.5 … 0.5, rounded to a 32-bit float as `TestEmbedding`'s `float[]` holds it), so equal texts give equal vectors (CTX-D-081 (5)); `prompt_eval_count` the token counts of all inputs summed.
